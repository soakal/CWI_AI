Client Testimonial Interview

8 camera questions designed to draw out authentic, compelling answers from your clients

8 questions ~10–15 min interview Guided format
Ask off-camera; edit to answers only
Before the Interview
Interviewer setup notes: — Ask the client to look at the interviewer, not the camera lens. — Have them state their name, business, and what they do before the first question — this becomes your intro title card. — Tell them: "There are no wrong answers. If you stumble, just restart the sentence. We'll edit it cleanly." — Film at least two takes of any answer that feels hesitant. — Do NOT read questions on-camera. You ask, they answer — only the answers appear in the final cut.
8 Interview Questions
1
Before you started using CWI AI, what was the biggest frustration you had with managing leads or customer calls?
This sets up the "before" state. You want them to describe a specific pain — missing calls, losing jobs, being overwhelmed. Let them be honest and even a little emotional about the stress it caused.
Follow-up if they're vague: "Can you think of a specific time a missed call cost you a job? What happened?"
2
What made you decide to look for a solution — and why did you choose CWI AI over other options?
This builds credibility by showing they did their homework. "I tried other things and this worked" is gold. Let them talk through their decision process without interrupting.
Follow-up: "Was there anything that almost stopped you from moving forward?"
3
What surprised you most about how CWI AI actually works once you set it up?
This is where you get the "wow" moments — customers being surprised by how natural the AI sounds, how fast the response is, how easy setup was. Authentic surprise is your most compelling marketing asset.
Follow-up: "Did any of your customers mention the AI? What did they say?"
4
Walk me through a specific example of a job or customer that came directly from CWI AI — one that you might have missed before.
Specifics sell. "We got a $1,200 emergency call at 11pm that booked itself" is worth more than any general statement. Coach them gently: "Take your time — give us the details."
Follow-up: "What time of day was it? How did you find out about it in the morning?"
5
What results have you seen since you started using CWI AI — in terms of leads, bookings, or revenue?
Push for numbers if they have them. "More leads" is okay; "30% more booked jobs in the first month" is a video clip. If they don't have exact numbers, "I feel like we're capturing twice as many leads" still works well.
Follow-up: "If you had to put a dollar amount on what CWI AI has meant for your business this year, what would it be?"
6
How has using CWI AI changed the way you spend your time day-to-day?
This gets at the lifestyle benefit — less stress, more time with family, not being glued to the phone. Emotional answers here are powerful. Let them tell the story of what their day looks like now versus before.
Follow-up: "Has anything changed outside of the business — in your personal life or stress level?"
7
What would you tell another service business owner who is on the fence about trying AI for their business?
This is your peer-to-peer close. Business owners trust other business owners. "Just try it" or "I wish I had done this two years ago" are the kinds of direct, quotable lines that end videos perfectly.
Follow-up: "What's the one thing you'd want them to know before they decide?"
8
Is there anything else you want people watching this to know about your experience with CWI AI?
Open-ended closer. Sometimes the best quote of the whole interview comes here — things they thought of but didn't have a question to answer. Always ask this even if the previous answers were great. You might be surprised.
Closing prompt: "Last thing — can you give us your name, your business, and where people can find you?" This becomes the end card.

Lighting Tips

  • Use natural light first. Position the subject facing a window — free, flattering, and professional.
  • Add a key light. A $40 ring light or softbox eliminates shadows under the eyes and chin.
  • Avoid mixed color temps. Don't mix daylight windows with warm indoor bulbs. Match or cover one source.
  • Background matters. A clean, slightly blurred background (f/2.8 or portrait mode) keeps focus on the speaker.
  • Check your exposure. If the viewer has to squint or lean in to see the subject's face, relight before rolling.

Audio Tips

  • Lapel mic beats camera mic. A $30 lav mic makes your video sound 10x more professional than any built-in microphone.
  • Kill the HVAC. Turn off air conditioning and heating units during recording — they create a constant hum that AI cleanup can't fully fix.
  • Record a room tone. Capture 10 seconds of silence in the room before the interview. Invaluable for audio editing.
  • Monitor while recording. Use headphones plugged into the audio source to catch pops or dropouts before you lose the take.
  • Speak up, slow down. Nervousness causes people to rush and drop volume. Remind subjects: slower is more confident on camera.

Thumbnail Best Practices

  • Face + emotion wins. Human faces with clear emotion (surprise, relief, confidence) consistently outperform text-only or product thumbnails.
  • 5-word max text overlay. If viewers need to read more than 5 words, the thumbnail is too complicated. Cut it down.
  • Use the #FF5A1F accent. A consistent brand color in your thumbnails builds recognition across your channel and ads.
  • Design at 1280×720. Check legibility at small sizes — your thumbnail must read clearly at 168×94 (YouTube mobile).
  • A/B test every thumbnail. Use YouTube Studio or Facebook Ads Manager split testing. The difference can be 3–4× click-through rate.
  • Avoid clickbait. Thumbnails that over-promise and under-deliver tank watch time and hurt algorithmic ranking.
