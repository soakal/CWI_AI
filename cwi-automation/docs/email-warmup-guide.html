Why Email Warmup Matters

Build Sender Reputation Before You Send at Scale

When you add a new domain to Resend (or any ESP), inbox providers like Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo have zero history on your sending behavior. They cannot tell whether you are a legitimate sender or a spammer. Without a warmup period, blasting thousands of emails from day one almost always triggers spam-filter action — bulk sends get deferred, your domain lands on blocklists, and reversing that damage can take months.

Email warmup is the practice of gradually increasing daily send volume over 30 days while maintaining healthy engagement metrics (high opens, low bounces, near-zero spam complaints). This trains receiving mail servers to recognize your domain as a trustworthy source and allocates you a larger sending "budget" over time.

During warmup, focus on your most engaged contacts first — people who opted in recently and are likely to open. Every open is a positive reputation signal. Every bounce or spam complaint is a negative one. The thresholds below reflect Resend's recommended limits and Google/Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

📧
Send to engaged lists only
Use contacts who have recently opened or clicked. Avoid cold lists during warmup.
📊
Monitor daily
Check bounce and spam rates every day. One bad batch can stall the entire warmup.
⚠️
Pause if rates spike
If bounces exceed 2% or spam exceeds 0.1%, stop sending and investigate immediately.
🧹
Clean your list first
Run all contacts through an email validation service before warmup begins.

DNS Records Checklist
SPF Record
Authorizes Resend to send on your behalf
Type: TXT
Name: @ (or your domain root)
Value: v=spf1 include:amazonses.com ~all
DKIM Record
Signs outbound mail to prevent spoofing
Type: CNAME
Name: resend._domainkey
Value: resend._domainkey.resend.com
DMARC Record
Policy for handling authentication failures
Type: TXT
Name: _dmarc
Value: v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com
MX Record
Routes inbound mail (for bounce handling)
Type: MX
Name: @ (or your sending subdomain)
Priority: 10
Value: feedback-smtp.us-east-1.amazonses.com
Sending Subdomain CNAME
Custom tracking & sending endpoint
Type: CNAME
Name: send.yourdomain.com
Value: eu-mail.resend.com
Resend Health Check Steps
1
Verify Domain in Resend Dashboard
Log in to resend.com/domains and confirm your domain shows Verified status. All DNS records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) must show green checkmarks. If any record is pending, re-check propagation — DNS changes can take up to 48 hours.
Resend Domains Docs →
2
Send a Test Email
From the Resend dashboard or via API, send a test email to your own inbox (Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail) using your verified sending domain. Confirm the email lands in inbox — not spam. Check that the From address, subject line, and HTML render correctly. Verify the unsubscribe header is present.
Resend Send Docs →
3
Check Spam Score with Mail Tester
Go to mail-tester.com and get a unique test address. Send an actual campaign-style email (with HTML body, unsubscribe link) to that address, then view your score. Target 9/10 or higher. Common deductions: missing List-Unsubscribe header, broken HTML, or a DMARC policy of p=none.
mail-tester.com →
4
Review Resend Logs & Webhook Events
In the Resend dashboard navigate to Logs and review delivery events for any bounced, complained, or failed entries. Set up a webhook endpoint in your app to receive email.bounced and email.complained events so you can automatically suppress those addresses.
Resend Logs Docs →
5
Check Google Postmaster Tools
Register your domain at postmaster.google.com. After a few days of sending, you will see Domain Reputation and IP Reputation scores. Aim for HIGH or MEDIUM. A LOW or BAD score means Gmail is already filtering your mail — pause sending and investigate bounce/complaint sources.
