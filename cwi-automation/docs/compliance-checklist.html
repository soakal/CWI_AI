Section 01 High Severity

HIPAA — The One That Can Bite Hard

The moment your chatbot or automation touches patient health information — a name tied to an appointment, a symptom, insurance details, anything — it is handling Protected Health Information (PHI). Under HIPAA you become a "Business Associate" of that healthcare client, and the entire chain of vendors must be HIPAA-compliant with signed Business Associate Agreements (BAAs).

Core Rule: PHI in the pipeline = Business Associate status = signed BAAs required across every vendor that touches the data. One unsigned BAA in the chain breaks HIPAA compliance for the entire setup.

Section 02 Medium Severity

Business Entity & Personal Liability Protection

Operating as a sole proprietor means a client dispute can come after your personal assets — house, savings, car. An LLC creates a legal wall between the business and you personally. Do this before signing your first client.

What to Set Up
  • Form an LLC in Michigan — file with LARA (Michigan Dept. of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs). ~$50 filing fee. Do it yourself online or use a service (~$100–300).
  • Get an EIN from the IRS — free, takes 10 minutes online. Required for business banking and taxes.
  • Open a business checking account under the LLC — not your personal name. Keeps the legal wall intact.
  • Keep personal and business finances fully separate — mixing them ("piercing the corporate veil") destroys the liability protection the LLC gives you.
  • Consider an operating agreement (even single-member) — some banks require it, and it reinforces the liability shield.
Cost Reality
~$50–350 one-time + $25/yr

Filing fee + optional service fee. Plus $25/year Michigan annual report. Cheap insurance against losing personal assets. Do this before signing your first client.

Section 03 Medium Severity

Insurance

Talk to a local commercial insurance broker. You need three types of coverage before you start scaling — at 2–3 clients, not 10.

E&O / Professional Liability

Covers you if your service causes a client financial harm — e.g., the bot gives wrong info, a booking failure costs them business. This is the big one for a service business touching client operations.

General Liability

Basic business coverage. Required by some clients before they'll sign a contract. Relatively inexpensive, often bundled with E&O.

Cyber Liability

Covers data breach response, notification costs, legal fees. Increasingly important since you handle customer data flowing through multiple platforms.

~$500–1,500/year combined

Typical range for a solo digital service business — E&O + general + cyber bundled. Get quotes once you have your first client or two. Don't skip it before scaling. Prioritize E&O and cyber.

Section 04 Low Severity

AI Disclosure

A growing number of states require businesses to disclose when a customer is interacting with an AI bot rather than a human. Even where not legally required, it's an ethical best practice that builds trust. This is cheap and easy — bake the disclosure into every bot's welcome message from day one and you're 90% covered.

What to Do (Easy)
Welcome Message Template
"Hi! I'm [Business]'s virtual assistant (an AI). I can help with scheduling, hours, and common questions. For anything else, I'll connect you with the team."
  • When escalating to a human, make the handoff explicit: "Connecting you with a real team member now."
  • Add a one-line AI disclosure near the chat widget on the client's website if their state requires it.
  • Verify current AI disclosure requirements for Michigan (and any state a client operates in) — these laws are changing fast in 2026.
This is a one-time template update. Bake the disclosure language into every bot's welcome message from day one — it covers you legally and sets accurate expectations for end users.
Section 05 Low Severity

Data Privacy — General (Non-HIPAA)

Even for non-healthcare clients you're handling customer PII — names, emails, phone numbers. Cover yourself with a few simple steps that cost nothing to implement.

What to Ensure
  • Contract states clients own their data and you don't sell it — keep this clause and verify it's in every agreement.
  • Add a simple privacy note to each client's chat widget: "By chatting, you agree your info may be used to respond to your inquiry. We never sell your data."
  • Honor deletion requests — if a customer asks the client to delete their data, you need a process to do it (even a manual one is fine to start).
  • GDPR: Only applies if you take clients with EU customers. Flag for later — not a day-one concern for local Michigan businesses.
